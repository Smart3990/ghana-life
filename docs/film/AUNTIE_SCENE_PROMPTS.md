# Ghanaverse film, part 2: the kelewele auntie

Comes straight after the thief gets away ("You lost him… He is gone, into the crowd") and ends on Uncle Kwame's phone call. Nine short shots, about 75 seconds in all.

**How to make it:**
1. For each shot, first make the **start picture** with an image generator (16:9), using the character sheet so the people look the same in every shot.
2. Then make the **video** with image-to-video: upload that picture as the first frame, and paste the **style block**, the **character sheet** and the shot's **video prompt** together.
3. Settings: 16:9, 1080p (720p for phones), 24 fps, 6 to 10 seconds per shot.
4. **Voices:** if the video tool makes speech (for example Veo 3.1), keep the spoken lines in the prompt. If it doesn't, the game adds the voices and subtitles. Subtitles are never drawn into the video.

All people and places are invented. No logos, brand names, readable signs, weapons or real people. No real phone numbers are ever shown or said.

---

## Style block (paste at the start of every shot)

Photorealistic cinematic film, shot on 35mm film with an anamorphic lens, natural film grain. Late afternoon turning to golden hour in central Accra, Ghana: warm low sun, soft haze, dust and charcoal smoke catching the light. Rich, natural colours; deep brown skin rendered with real texture and warmth. Slow, smooth, steady camera (dolly or gimbal); no shaking, no sudden zooms, no cuts inside the shot. People move naturally, with weight, breathing and small gestures; faces, hair and clothes stay exactly the same from the first frame to the last. Emotional and warm: a stranger's kindness in a busy city. No text, no subtitles, no logos, no watermarks.

## Character sheet (paste with every shot)

- **The newcomer (you):** a Ghanaian young man of about 20, slim, short black hair, a dark navy zip jacket over a plain light-grey T-shirt, dark trousers, worn trainers. He has **no bag now** (it was stolen): his shoulder is empty and his hands keep going to where the strap was. Dusty, sweaty from running, eyes worried but polite.
- **The kelewele auntie (Auntie Dede):** a Ghanaian woman of about 50, round, kind face, strong arms, a bright orange-and-green wax-print dress and matching head wrap tied high, a faded apron over it, small gold earrings. Loud, warm, motherly; she laughs easily and scolds with love.
- **Her stall:** a wooden table under a big faded red-and-yellow beach umbrella at the edge of the station. A charcoal coal pot with a wide black frying pan of hot oil; cubes of ripe plantain sizzling, turning golden. A tray of cut plantain, a bowl of ginger and pepper paste, a stack of small paper wraps and clear plastic bags, a plastic bucket of water. Two plastic chairs (one blue, one red) beside the table. Smoke and steam rising into the sunlight.
- **Uncle Kwame:** a Ghanaian man of about 55, grey at the temples, short beard, reading glasses pushed up on his forehead, a short-sleeved checked shirt with a pen in the pocket, a calm, wise face that hides a lot of worry.
- **Background:** the busy station: white minibuses (trotros), taxis with yellow-painted corners, hawkers with head trays, umbrella stalls, a concrete road flyover far behind.

## Shot 1: You lost him (7 s)

**Start picture prompt:** The newcomer from the character sheet stands at the edge of a crowded Accra lorry station, bent over with his hands on his knees, out of breath, his empty shoulder towards the camera. Behind him the crowd closes up where the thief disappeared; trotros, taxis, women with head trays, dust in the golden light.

**Video prompt:** The camera starts at waist height behind the newcomer and slowly circles round to his side. 0 to 2 s: he breathes hard, chest heaving, sweat on his neck. 2 to 4 s: he straightens up, scans the crowd left and right, then touches his empty shoulder where the bag strap was; realisation hits him. 4 to 6 s: he puts both hands on his head and lets out a long breath; people walk past without noticing. 6 to 7 s: he looks down at his empty hands. Hold.

**Sound:** the station roar, horns, a bus mate calling "Kaneshie! Kaneshie!", his heavy breathing.

**Avoid:** the thief reappearing, the bag appearing, tears too strong, people walking through each other.

## Shot 2: The auntie calls out (7 s)

**Start picture prompt:** Over the newcomer's shoulder, a few metres away at the edge of the station, Auntie Dede's kelewele stall under a big faded red-and-yellow umbrella: smoke rising from the coal pot, plantain sizzling in a wide black pan. She has stopped frying and is looking straight at the newcomer with concern, spoon in one hand, the other hand on her hip.

**Video prompt:** The camera slowly pushes past the newcomer's shoulder towards the stall. 0 to 2 s: oil bubbles, golden plantain cubes sizzle, smoke curls up through the sunbeams. 2 to 4 s: Auntie Dede lifts her head and sees him; her face changes from busy to worried. 4 to 7 s: she waves her spoon and beckons him over with her whole arm, calling out. The newcomer turns towards her.

**Spoken (Auntie, loud, warm Ghanaian English):** "Hey! My son! Come, come here!"

**Sound:** sizzling oil, the crackle of charcoal, station noise.

**Avoid:** readable writing on the umbrella, food changing shape, extra arms.

## Shot 3: "Ei! Sit down, my child" (8 s)

**Start picture prompt:** A medium shot beside the stall. Auntie Dede pulls a blue plastic chair out from beside her table with one hand, looking up at the newcomer, who stands in front of her, dusty and lost. The coal pot smokes between them.

**Video prompt:** Side-on shot at seated height; slow dolly to the left. 0 to 2 s: she drags the plastic chair across the ground towards him; it scrapes. 2 to 5 s: she taps the seat twice and points at it, speaking. 5 to 8 s: he hesitates, then sits down slowly, elbows on knees; she leans towards him, studying his face.

**Spoken (Auntie):** "Ei! Sit down, my child. What happened?"

**Sound:** the chair scraping, oil sizzling, her voice cutting through the noise.

**Avoid:** the chair floating, the newcomer sitting through the chair, his clothes changing.

## Shot 4: "A boy took my bag" (8 s)

**Start picture prompt:** A close-up of the newcomer sitting on the blue plastic chair, looking up at the auntie, his hands open in front of him, his face dusty and upset. Soft smoke and the blurred station behind him.

**Video prompt:** Slow push-in on the newcomer's face. 0 to 3 s: he swallows, looks away towards the crowd where the boy ran. 3 to 6 s: he turns back to her and speaks, his voice breaking a little, opening his empty hands. 6 to 8 s: he shakes his head and stares at the ground.

**Spoken (You, quiet, shaky):** "A boy took my bag. My money, my phone… everything."

**Avoid:** overacting, tears pouring, the face changing.

---

## Shot 5: "Sorry, ooo" (9 s)

**Start picture prompt:** Auntie Dede, in a medium close-up, scooping a small portion of golden kelewele out of the pan into a paper wrap, her face full of sympathy, looking at the newcomer.

**Video prompt:** Slow arc around the auntie from her side to her front. 0 to 3 s: she clicks her tongue and shakes her head slowly, scooping hot kelewele into a paper wrap. 3 to 5 s: she sprinkles a pinch of roasted groundnuts on top and holds it out to him. 5 to 9 s: she speaks gently while he takes the warm paper with both hands; steam rises from it.

**Spoken (Auntie, soft and motherly):** "Sorry, ooo. Eat small. Who do you know in Accra?"

**Sound:** the paper rustling, the sizzle in the background.

**Avoid:** the food turning into something else, hands deforming, the wrap floating.

---

## Shot 6: "My uncle. Uncle Kwame." (7 s)

**Start picture prompt:** A two-shot from the side: the newcomer on the blue chair holding the paper of kelewele, the auntie standing over him with one hand on her hip, both lit by the low sun, smoke between them.

**Video prompt:** Static camera with a very slow push-in. 0 to 2 s: he eats one piece of kelewele; for a moment the warmth helps. 2 to 5 s: he looks up at her with a little hope and answers. 5 to 7 s: she nods firmly, satisfied, already wiping her hands on her apron.

**Spoken (You):** "My uncle. Uncle Kwame."

**Avoid:** chewing that looks wrong, the food vanishing, extra people stepping between them.

---

## Shot 7: She calls him on her phone (9 s)

**Start picture prompt:** A close shot of Auntie Dede holding a small, old, scratched basic phone with physical buttons, wrapped in a rubber band, close to her face, her thumb on the keys, the newcomer blurred beside her. No screen text visible.

**Video prompt:** Slow push-in on the auntie and the phone. 0 to 3 s: she pulls the phone from her apron pocket and speaks to the newcomer without looking at him. 3 to 6 s: he says the number quietly (not heard clearly); she taps the keys with her thumb, lips moving as she repeats the digits under her breath. 6 to 9 s: she lifts the phone to her ear and waits, eyebrows raised, looking at him; we hear the ringing tone.

**Spoken (Auntie):** "Give me his number. I will call him on my phone."

**Sound:** button beeps, a ringing tone.

**Avoid:** readable numbers or screen text, a modern smartphone, the phone changing shape.

---

## Shot 8: Uncle Kwame answers (8 s)

**Start picture prompt:** Uncle Kwame from the character sheet, in the yard of an old compound house elsewhere in Accra at golden hour: washing on a line, a water drum, a door with a faded curtain. He is answering a basic phone at his ear, reading glasses on his forehead, a newspaper folded in his other hand.

**Video prompt:** Slow push-in from a medium shot to a close-up of Uncle Kwame. 0 to 2 s: he answers casually, still reading the newspaper. 2 to 5 s: he hears the news; he lowers the newspaper, takes off his glasses, his face turns serious and worried. 5 to 8 s: he stands up from his bench, already moving, and speaks firmly and calmly into the phone.

**Spoken (Uncle Kwame, deep, calm, Ghanaian English):** "Stay there. I'm coming."

**Sound:** a radio playing highlife softly in the yard, a chicken, children far away.

**Avoid:** readable signs, the yard changing, the phone becoming a smartphone.

---

## Shot 9: Waiting at Circle (10 s, the ending)

**Start picture prompt:** A wide shot of the kelewele stall at sunset: the newcomer sitting on the blue plastic chair eating kelewele from the paper, the auntie back at her pan frying and laughing with a customer, the big umbrella glowing in the low sun; behind them the busy station and the concrete flyover in golden haze.

**Video prompt:** The camera starts at the stall and cranes slowly up and back, rising above the umbrella so the whole busy station and the flyover open up in the sunset. 0 to 3 s: the auntie says something to the newcomer and points the spoon at him; he smiles for the first time. 3 to 7 s: the camera rises; trotros edge through the crowd, hawkers weave between taxis, smoke from the stall drifts up past the lens. 7 to 10 s: a wide golden view of Circle at dusk, the first lights coming on; hold still for the last second (the game shows the chapter card here).

**Spoken (Auntie, laughing):** "Don't worry. Accra will not swallow you. Eat!"

**Sound:** the station softening into evening, highlife from a kiosk speaker, the sizzle of the pan.

**Avoid:** text, the stall changing, people merging, sudden camera shake.
